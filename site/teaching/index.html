Graduate course

CE 513

Game Theory for Interactive Autonomy

  • Game theory
  • Multi-agent decisions
  • Interactive autonomy

Course overview

What should an intelligent agent do when everyone else is making decisions too? Designed by Ruolin Li, this graduate course connects game theory with AI, robotics, and autonomous systems. Students examine competition, cooperation, and incentives—and how individual choices shape collective outcomes.

Who it’s for

For graduate students interested in strategic decision-making across civil engineering, computer science, robotics, control, and related fields. Bring your disciplinary perspective and an application you care about. Students from other programs can contact Ruolin to discuss fit and preparation.

What to expect

Learn to formulate interactions as games, reason about equilibria and incentives, and critically connect theoretical models to autonomous systems. Expect to move between mathematical reasoning, concrete examples, and open research questions.

Guest lectures · Research at the frontier

Share frontier research, a recent result, or an open problem in game theory and interactive autonomy. Help graduate students see where foundational ideas meet unresolved questions—and where their own research could begin.

Undergraduate course

CE 471

Principles of Transportation Engineering

  • Transportation systems
  • Traffic operations
  • Mobility planning

Course overview

Why do queues form, how do networks move people, and what makes a transportation system work well? This undergraduate course introduces transportation planning, design, and operations, connecting engineering fundamentals to the streets, intersections, and mobility systems people use every day.

Who it’s for

For undergraduates building a foundation in transportation engineering, particularly students in civil and environmental engineering. Students in related programs who are curious about mobility can contact Ruolin to discuss course fit; enrollment follows USC requirements.

What to expect

Build the vocabulary and analytical perspective to understand transportation problems, evaluate engineering choices, and connect classroom ideas to real systems. Explore what careers in industry, public agencies, technology, and academic research can look like.

Guest lectures · Where can transportation take you?

Bring transportation careers to life through a real project and an honest account of your path. Practitioners, public-sector professionals, researchers, and mobility technologists are welcome—including early-career speakers who can help students imagine their next steps.
